If you're named

If you're a councillor, or you speak for a council, and something this site says about you is wrong or unfair, or you'd like to say something in your own words, this is what you can do.

Who this is for

  • Councillors named in the site's named votes, lists of members and briefings, including mayors and London Assembly members.
  • Councils and strategic authorities, as bodies: what the site says about who controls them, their members, their meetings and their votes.
  • Someone acting for them, such as a party group's office, a council's democratic services team or its press office.

If you're named in council minutes but you're not a councillor (you spoke at a meeting, presented a petition or work for the council, say), your name should already have been removed. If it hasn't, or something about you should not be shown, send a takedown request. These are dealt with first.

What it covers

Everything the site says about a councillor or a council, not only briefings:

  • a councillor's party on the date of a meeting, as the council recorded it, and the party it was matched to;
  • named votes: which side each councillor is shown on, and whether the name in the minutes was matched to the right member;
  • a council's control (the party in overall control, or no overall control) and, for a strategic authority, its mayor's party, shown in its place;
  • members by party, a member's role on a strategic authority, and the party taken from their home council;
  • connections the site draws: between a strategic authority and its councils, and to a council's pages on Where Landlords Stand;
  • coverage labels on the list of councils ("full", "partial" and "not yet") and the reasons given for them;
  • how briefings group councils: by the party in overall control, by region, or by kind of council;
  • briefings, and what they say the minutes record.

Mistakes in the minutes themselves can only be corrected by the council: contact its democratic services team.

Three routes

  1. Something is wrong

    A fact, a date, a vote, a party, a label or a connection is incorrect, or a briefing misstates the minutes. We correct it or withdraw it as soon as we can, and say publicly what changed and when, in the corrections log.

    Tell us what's wrong

  2. You've been characterised unfairly

    The record is right, but the site's own words, labels or groupings are unfair: a loaded word in the site's own voice, a connection without published evidence, or a grouping that suggests something the minutes don't show. We check it against Where They Stand's impartiality policy and, if it breaks the policy, put it right in the same way.

    Tell us what's unfair

  3. Add a statement in your own words

    The record is accurate and the site has followed the policy, but you see it differently. You can add a short statement, shown beside the item.

    Add a statement

Statements in your own words

  • What it can say: up to 150 words, in your own name, about the item it sits beside: a named vote, a council's page or a briefing. Point to the record where you can (the council's minutes, a published statement), with a link.
  • What it can't contain: anything defamatory or abusive; anything about a private individual, or personal information about anyone else; appeals for votes, money or support; or comments on someone else's conduct. During elections, statements are still accepted, but not ones that campaign.
  • How it is shown: beside the item, and on the council's page, headed "Statement from [your name], [date]" and set apart from the site's own text, as below. It is published as you wrote it, or declined with our reasons. It is never edited, except to take out something these rules don't allow, with your agreement.
  • Never part of the record: it is never given to the AI model that writes briefings, never searched when a briefing is written, and never treated as part of the council's record.
  • Changing it: you can update or withdraw it at any time, by emailing admin@wherecouncilsstand.com from the address you gave. We can remove a statement that breaks these rules, and we'll tell you why.

How we check who you are

Before a statement is published, or changed or withdrawn at your request, we confirm that it comes from you or someone acting for you. We write to the email address you give: an address at your council, or your party group's official address, is quickest. If you use another, we may ask you to confirm from an address your council publishes. Reports that something is wrong or unfair don't need this.

The address is used only for this. It is kept while your statement is shown, and deleted three months after it is withdrawn or declined (see privacy).

How long it takes

We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days.

Anything that may be unlawful or harmful, and takedown requests about people who aren't councillors, are dealt with sooner. If a concern looks well founded but needs time to decide, we may hide the item meanwhile, with a note.

Who decides, and appeals

Where They Stand's founder decides, until its trustees appoint someone else. Every concern, the decision and its reasons are logged, and the trustees review the log at each meeting.

If you're unhappy with a decision, you can ask for a review (by the trustees, once they are appointed): email admin@wheretheystand.uk. Once the charity is registered, if you're still unhappy, you can also raise concerns about it with the Charity Commission.

Letters claiming legal liability, such as defamation, go straight to the trustees. Disputed material may be taken down while they take advice.
