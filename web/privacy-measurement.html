Privacy

This site shows no advertising and needs no account. It sets no cookies and measures nothing unless you agree.

Where They Stand, a charitable incorporated organisation registered in England and Wales (charity number NNNNNNN), is the data controller.

Visitors

  • Each briefing is saved with its question, so it can be shared at its own address, answered again without paying for a new briefing, and checked against exactly what was shown if someone complains. Saved briefings aren’t listed anywhere and aren’t linked to the person who asked. For each briefing the site also notes how long each step took, what it cost and what the automatic checks found, so that slow or poorly checked briefings can be found and fixed. These notes hold nothing that identifies you; they are kept for 13 months, and the question of a briefing that could not be written is removed from them after 90 days. Please don’t put personal details in a question.
  • To apply the daily limit on new briefings and prevent abuse, your IP address is turned into a scrambled daily code. The secret used to make it changes every day and the old one is deleted, so the code can’t be linked to the next day’s or turned back into an address. The address itself isn’t stored.
  • A problem report or takedown request is saved, with your email address if you choose to give one. The address is used only to reply, and is deleted when the report is dealt with. If you agreed to measurement (below), Google is told only that a report was sent, never what it says or who sent it.
  • Under the right of reply, a councillor or a council (or someone acting for them) can add a short statement beside what this site says about them (If you’re named). The statement is published with their name and role, as they wrote it, and is never sent to the AI model. The email address used to confirm who they are, and to let them update or withdraw it, is kept while the statement is waiting or shown, and deleted three months after it is withdrawn, declined or removed.
  • Backups of the site’s database are kept for up to two months, so a deleted address can remain in a backup until then.
  • Your question and the minutes found are sent to Anthropic, which provides the AI model that writes briefings. Under Anthropic’s terms for its API, they aren’t used to train its models.
  • Cloudflare carries visits to the site. It passes each visit to the server and shields the site from floods of automated requests. It sees each request as it passes, including your IP address, possibly outside the UK, under Cloudflare’s privacy policy. Its analytics and visitor tracking are turned off for this site. If Cloudflare ever has to check that a visitor isn’t an automated program (during an attack, say), it may set a short-lived security cookie of its own for that purpose only.
  • The hosting provider runs the server and has no access to the site’s data beyond keeping it running. Requests aren’t logged.

Measuring visits for Google’s advertising grant

Where They Stand advertises its sites on Google Search with Google Ad Grants, free advertising that Google gives charities. To keep the grant, it has to show Google that the ads lead people to use the sites. So, only if you agree, this site uses the Google tag to tell Google when you finish reading a briefing (when the end of it comes into view), open a source or a council’s own minutes, follow the Donate link, or send a report or a statement. That is all it tells Google about: no list of the pages you read, and never your questions or anything else you type.

Nothing is loaded from Google until you choose. A box at the top of the page asks you, with “Accept” and “Reject”. If you reject, or don’t choose, nothing is loaded from Google and no cookie is set. If you accept, your browser loads the Google tag from Google, which sets cookies whose names start “_gcl_” on this site, and sends Google your IP address, details of your browser, the address of the page and the action. Google uses them to count how many visits from its ads led to that action.

Google receives this information as an independent controller, for its own purposes, under the Google Ads Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms. How it uses it is set out in Google’s privacy policy and on its Business Data Responsibility page. Google says its “_gcl_” cookies last for 90 days (how Google uses cookies), and explains how long it keeps what it collects in how Google retains data.

The legal basis is your consent. We keep only your choice, and the day you made it, in your own browser (in its local storage, not in a cookie), and we never receive it. After a year you are asked again.

To change your mind, use “Measurement choices” at the foot of any page. Choosing “Reject” after accepting deletes Google’s cookies on this site and reloads the page with nothing from Google. Clearing this site’s data in your browser also removes them, and your choice.

No ads are ever shown on this site or on the charity’s other sites, and nothing on them is personalised. The Google tag is told not to use your visit to personalise ads.

People named in council records

Councillors are described using public information about their role: their party on the date, offices held, how they voted and what the minutes record them as saying, and interests they declared at meetings. This is processed in the public interest of reporting on how councils work.

Other people named in minutes (members of the public who speak, ask questions, present petitions, apply for planning permission or object, and council staff) are not identified. Their names are removed automatically before the minutes are searched, shown or sent to the AI model, and licensing hearings are not included. The removal is done by rules that look for the ways minutes name people, and it misses some names; if yours is shown, ask for it to be removed and it will be taken out by hand. Declarations of interest are given only as who declared, the item and the kind of interest: the reasons, which can describe a councillor’s family or health, are left to the council’s own minutes. Links go to the council’s own record, which remains the council’s responsibility.

If you’re a councillor, or you speak for a council, see If you’re named on this site. If you are named in the minutes but aren’t a councillor, and think something is wrong or should not be shown, tell us: takedown requests are dealt with first. The official record itself can only be corrected by the council.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of information held about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, object to its use, and withdraw your consent to measurement at any time, as above. Because the site doesn’t know who visitors are, we may need your help to find it, such as the address of a briefing or the date you sent a report. If you’re unhappy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.
