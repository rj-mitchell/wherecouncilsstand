# Decisions

Product decisions made by the owner. They override anything else in this repo. Add new decisions here once they're
made.

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name and owner | **Where Councils Stand**, a Where They Stand project from day one, with its own domain. Listed under "In development" in the Charity Commission application. |
| 2 | Coverage | Principal councils across the **UK**: England, Wales, and (the owner's decision, 5 November 2026) **Scotland and Northern Ireland**, whose minutes are published through Civica's **Modern.gov**, Civica's **CMIS** or Northgate's **CoINS** (CMIS and CoINS readers added at the owner's request, 5 November 2026). Councils on other systems are left out for now; the reason is published. Sites that refuse the crawler are asked, not worked around. Robots.txt isn't treated as a barrier to these public records (decision 21, which replaces the earlier rule that left out CMIS sites whose robots.txt forbids crawling; the owner asked on 6 November 2026 for those 26 councils to be read too). England's strategic authorities are covered too (decision 17). |
| 3 | Meetings | **Full council and cabinet** (or executive), including annual, budget and extraordinary council meetings and a mayor and cabinet. Not cabinet sub-committees, advisory panels or decisions by individual cabinet members. For councils run under a committee system, which have no cabinet: the committees their constitution gives the main policy and budget decisions, usually a policy and resources committee (`committees.py`, and `data_static/committees.csv` where a council's are named otherwise). A council that changed systems keeps whichever committees it had at the time. |
| 4 | How far back | **May 2018** for every council, so coverage is even. Each council's page shows its own coverage dates. Extending back to **25 February 2014**, when English councils had to start recording named budget votes, is the next step. |
| 5 | First users | Independent journalists and engaged members of the public. |
| 6 | Code | A new repo. Adapted from Where Countries Stand's and Where MPs Stand's code (same licence). |
| 7 | Neutral wording | The charity's impartiality policy applies. Minutes are officers' summaries, not verbatim records: briefings say "the minutes record that…" and never present a summary as a councillor's own words. |
| 8 | Party on the date | Each councillor is shown with the party recorded at that meeting, mapped to one code per party. Named local groups of independents or residents are local parties, not "Independent". |
| 9 | People named in council records | Briefings and pages are limited to councillors' own words and actions. Members of the public (speakers, petitioners, planning applicants and objectors) and council staff are not identified. Licensing hearings are not included. There is a published route for taking things down. |
| 10 | Reuse | Facts (votes, attendance, declarations, decisions) are extracted and published. Minutes are indexed in full but shown only as cited extracts linking to the council's own record. Each council's licence statement is recorded as it is added. |
| 11 | Being a good citizen | One request at a time to each council, at least a second apart, with an identifying user agent. Responses are cached so each meeting is fetched once. |
| 12 | Grouping evidence | Briefings group councils by facts recorded on the date, the same way for every party: by default the party in overall control (a party with more than half the members at full council), or no overall control; by region, kind of council or council when asked. Evidence is drawn from each group in turn, at most two items per council. |
| 13 | Default period | Briefings default to the last four years (the current council term), with an obvious control to widen it back to May 2018. |
| 14 | Models | As the sibling sites: Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes briefings, Claude Haiku 5.5 reads the question into filters, with server-side fallbacks on. |
| 15 | Vote accuracy in the alpha | About 1 named vote in 100 with a councillor on the wrong side (`docs/PILOT.md`, three samples of 100) is **accepted for the alpha** (6 November 2026, family plan decision 12). The methodology page says so plainly, with the figures. A test set of checked votes is built before the beta. |
| 16 | The mark | A **town hall** (clock tower, pediment, four columns and a gilt step) on a rounded Buckram-brown tile with a fine gilt edge in `#D9BC72`, in the manner of Where MPs Stand's mark. Chosen by the owner on 6 November 2026 from seven drawings (an open minute book, a closed minute book, the council chamber, a gavel, a named vote, the town hall, and sealed minutes). It must stay distinct from Where Landlords Stand's mark. `docs/DESIGN.md`, "The mark". |
| 17 | Strategic authorities | England's **strategic authorities** (the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026's term: combined authorities, combined county authorities and the Greater London Authority) are covered, named **"strategic authorities"** on the site, with each one's statutory kind (mayoral combined authority, combined county authority, and so on) on its page (the owner's decision, 6 November 2026). A **pilot** of the 16 whose meetings are on a Modern.gov web service that answers came first: Greater Manchester, West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, North East, West of England, Cambridgeshire and Peterborough, York and North Yorkshire, East Midlands, Greater Lincolnshire, Devon and Torbay, Lancashire (on Lancashire County Council's site), Sussex and Brighton (on East Sussex County Council's site), Cheshire and Warrington (on Cheshire West and Chester's, which answers the server though not every network; its committees are those named for it), and the Greater London Authority (added at the owner's request, 6 November 2026: the London Assembly's full meetings, Mayor's Question Time, and its Budget and Performance and GLA Oversight committees). The **four that publish their meetings as documents on their own websites** follow, read by a reader of those sites (`sources/docsite.py`; added at the owner's request, 6 November 2026): Tees Valley, Hull and East Yorkshire, Cumbria, and Hampshire and the Solent, **20 in all**, every strategic authority that exists. One created later is listed, with the reason, and added when a reader exists or its site lets the server in. Each is keyed by its ONS `E47` code (the Greater London Authority by London's region code, `E12000007`, as before; the four created in 2026 by a stated provisional key until ONS publishes one), and its page links both ways, with dates, to its constituent councils' pages (`data_static/strategic_authorities.csv`, `data_static/authority_councils.csv`, `data_static/mayors.csv`). |
| 18 | Strategic authorities' meetings | The authority's **main board or cabinet**, including budget meetings; **the mayor's decisions**, where they are published as decisions in its meetings system; and its **overview and scrutiny committees**. From **May 2018 or the authority's creation**, whichever is later. Chosen per authority in `data_static/committees.csv`, with the reason for each (`committees.py` for the rules). Not audit, standards, appointments, advisory boards, joint committees with other bodies or police and crime panels. |
| 19 | Control, and briefings | Strategic authorities are **in briefings** from the start. For one with a mayor, "control" is **the mayor's party on the date**, always labelled as the mayor's ("mayor: Labour", "authorities with a Labour mayor"), never mixed with councils' party in overall control. For one without a mayor (or between mayors), it is **"no single party"**: its members are appointed by its constituent councils, each with the same say, so no party is given control (the simplest honest rule: a majority of three appointed members isn't control). Filters: nation England, the region it sits in (Greater Lincolnshire spans two and is placed in the East Midlands, where most of its people live), and the type "strategic authority" (the same type as the Greater London Authority's, fixed in #35). A briefing's balance treats an authority as one body, like a council: at most two items from each. |
| 20 | Authorities' members | Board and committee members are shown with their **role** (the mayor, constituent member, non-constituent member, substitute, co-opted member) and their **party on the date from their home council's record**, where the site has it, otherwise from the authority's own record, saying which. **Only councillors, London Assembly members and the mayor are named** (Assembly Members are elected, so they are named as councillors are, with the GLA's record of their party; the owner's decision, 6 November 2026). Appointed members who aren't councillors (business board chairs, independent and co-opted members) are counted but not named, and their names are removed from the minutes, as decision 9 does for everyone who isn't a councillor. |
| 21 | Robots.txt | **Robots.txt isn't treated as a barrier** to councils' and authorities' public records (the owner's decision, 6 November 2026: "it's unreasonable for it to be a blanket barrier to open data"). One setting, `[robots] obey` in `config/settings.toml`, is **off**: no reader (Modern.gov, CMIS, CoINS, or the authorities' own websites) obeys robots.txt's rules. It can be switched back on, and the code that obeys them is kept and tested. Still, whatever the setting: any `Crawl-delay` a site asks for is honoured, the crawler names itself, one request at a time at least a second apart, and a site that refuses requests (a 403, a bot check) is asked, never worked around. A council marked `robots` in the registry by hand is not read, and anything stored for it is kept but withheld (none that is loaded is so marked). No robots.txt letters are sent. |
| 22 | Corrections and the right of reply | The charity's corrections and right-of-reply policy (impartiality policy, section 7, final for building on 12 November 2026) is built into the site, in the family's words (the charity's glossary). **An "If you're named" page** (`/named`, linked from every page's footer beside "Corrections", and from the corrections page as "If you're named on this site") for **councillors** (mayors and London Assembly members included), **councils and strategic authorities as bodies**, and anyone acting for them. It sets out the three routes, each with a way to start it: **"Something is wrong"** and **"You've been characterised unfairly"** through the report form (`/corrections?kind=error`, `?kind=unfair`), and **"Add a statement in your own words"** through its own form (`?kind=statement`). Members of the public keep the takedown route (`?kind=takedown`), dealt with first. **Corrections cover the site's labels, groupings and connections, not only briefings:** a councillor's party on the date, which side of a named vote they're shown on, a council's control (and an authority's mayor's party), members by party and an authority member's role and home-council party, the links between authorities and their councils and to Where Landlords Stand, coverage labels, and how briefings group councils. **Response times**, on the corrections page and the named page: "We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days." **Statements** (policy 7.2): up to 150 words, with the author's name and role, about one item (a council's page, a named vote or a saved briefing), an optional link to the record, and an email address to confirm who sent it. Held as waiting in the site's own database (`state.db`, table `wts_statements` since decision 24) until the owner has confirmed the sender, then published as written or declined with reasons; the author can have it updated or withdrawn at any time (`councils list-statements`, `publish-statement`, `decline-statement`, `update-statement`, `withdraw-statement`; `docs/OPERATIONS.md`). Shown after the item and on the council's page, headed "Statement from [name], [date]" and followed by "In their own words. Not checked or endorsed by Where Councils Stand.", in a dashed, sunk panel in the sans face, apart from the record's book face. Councillors have no pages of their own, so a councillor's statement goes beside a vote, a briefing or their council's page, and is gathered on the council's page. **Never part of the record:** statements are never given to the AI model, never in the search index, never in a page's description or structured data; the code that builds the record and briefings doesn't read the site's own database (`tests/test_statements.py`). The confirming address is kept while a statement is shown and **deleted three months after it is withdrawn or declined** (as each statement arrives, by every statement command, and nightly by `councils update`), as the privacy notice says. |
| 23 | Why use this rather than a chatbot? | Readers are told plainly why the site is worth using rather than a general AI chatbot, in the family's words (the charity's glossary, "Why use this rather than a chatbot?", 13 November 2026; the owner's brief for the family's shared code, phase 1b). **On the home page**, just under the question box: one line that is always shown, "Answers only from the official record, every point linked and checked.", and a `<details>` disclosure, "Why use this rather than a chatbot?", that opens to five one-line points (only the record; every point linked and checked; up to date; it says when there is nothing; balanced by design) and "Briefings here are written by AI too. The difference is what the AI is given and how it is checked.", with a link to How it works (`web/src/components/Why.tsx`). The question box never moves down for it: the opening paragraph's sentence "Every point in the briefing cites the council's own minutes" gave way to the line, so the page doesn't say it twice. **On the About and How it works pages**, the same points at more length and two more (records searched in full; open), from one file (`web/partials/why.html`). No product or company is named, and nothing is said about what others get wrong. **Every claim must be true of this site's code and settings**, and is checked again before it is changed: only the record (rule 1 of the instructions in `rag/prompts.py`; `rag/answer.py` sends the passages found and the question); the checks (the shared package's `wts_core.checks`, with this site's rules in `rag/checks.py`, since decision 24; and "sentences", not "any sentence", because a short join between points isn't flagged); every day (`deploy/councils-update.timer`); "No records found" (`rag/pipeline.py`, `rag/prompts.py`); councils run by different parties (`rank/evidence.py`, decision 12); from May 2018, for the councils covered (`config/settings.toml`, decisions 2 and 4); open, free and no tracking (the How it works, terms and privacy pages). Nothing is counted when the disclosure is opened; a count, with no reader tracking, comes with the per-briefing statistics. |
| 24 | The shared package, the corrections log, and hiding a page | The site runs on **wts-core**, the Where They Stand sites' shared package (the owner's brief for the family's shared code, phase 4; 14 November 2026), installed as the wheel of a tagged release with its `cli`, `offsite` and `ai` extras. The package now does what this site did in its own copies of the sister sites' code: the settings loader, the state database and its backups, limits and spending, the answer cache and saved briefings, the flow of a question, the checks on briefings, reports and statements, the model client, the embedders and the search index's store. **What is this site's own stays here:** the councils' record and how it is read, the search and its balance across councils, the instructions to the model, the words on every page and in every message, the limits and prices (`config/settings.toml`), and the site's honorifics, evidence-note rule and list of loaded words (`rag/checks.py`). **Readers see no change but those listed here**, and tests hold the site to what it gave before, recorded from the old code (`tests/golden/`, `tests/test_parity.py`, `tests/test_import.py`). **The checks on briefings never weaken.** The state database's rows are carried over once, at the first start, with a copy made first and a way back (`docs/OPERATIONS.md`, "The state database"). **Two things the site gains** (the impartiality policy, section 7). **A public corrections log**, on the corrections page and at `/api/corrections`: each withdrawn briefing with the reason given, and each report closed with a note written for the log, newest first. It never shows a withdrawn briefing's question (a briefing may be withdrawn because its question names someone who isn't a councillor), anything a reporter wrote, or why a statement was removed. **Hiding a page while a concern is decided** (policy 7.3, which the "If you're named" page already promised): `councils hide <address> --note "…"` and `councils unhide`. A named vote, a council's (or strategic authority's) page or a saved briefing can be hidden, and nothing else; the page shows "This page is hidden while a concern about it is decided." and the note, and the item is left out of lists, the sitemap, search and the sources of briefings written meanwhile (what each kind is left out of is set out in `docs/OPERATIONS.md`, "Hiding a page"). A briefing saved before a page was hidden still shows the sources it was written from. **Also new, for the owner:** a record of each briefing (how it ended, how long it took, what it cost, what the checks found; nothing that identifies a reader; kept 13 months), `councils stats`, a weekly report with alert thresholds that start unset, a nightly `councils tidy` that deletes what the privacy notice says is deleted, `councils restore`, `councils eval`, and `/api/check` for the family's nightly check. **The privacy notice** gains the three sentences the owner accepted on 13 November 2026 (the record of each briefing; a statement's address kept while it is waiting or shown and deleted three months after it is withdrawn, declined or removed; backups kept for up to two months). **What the site does not do:** it keeps no separate note of failed questions, counts no reader's reports or statements (no visitor's code is kept with either), and sends no token counts to the reader. Statements carry `data-nosnippet`, so a search engine never quotes one as the site's own words. Commands for reports and statements have the family's names (`councils reports list`, `councils statements publish`); the names they had still work. |
